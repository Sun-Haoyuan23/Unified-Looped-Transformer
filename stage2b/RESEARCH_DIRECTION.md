# Dual-Clock Visual Loops in Hybrid Autoregressive–Flow Models

Status: experimental direction, 2026-10-03. Numerical claims below are from the current Show-o2 1.5B pilot, not a paper result.

## Central question

A hybrid model already iterates along two axes: autoregressive text/semantic computation and the visual flow trajectory. Repeating shared visual Transformer blocks adds a third source of iteration *inside each flow evaluation*. The scientific question is where that extra visual computation helps image quality and compositional correctness, and how to allocate it without weakening language or image understanding.

The proposed paper should be about **quality-directed computation allocation in a unified generator**, rather than only compressing a diffusion backbone. A convincing result must improve GenEval and human-perceived quality at a measured compute budget, preserve diversity, and explicitly test understanding.

## Method under development

1. **Recurrent visual core and placement.** Compare three ten-call layouts at a fixed 50-step sampler: `CYCLE` (five blocks, then the same five again), `SEQUENCE` (five adjacent repeated pairs), and `MIDDLE` (two independent prefix blocks, three repeated middle blocks, two independent suffix blocks). Shared attention, MLP, and modulation matrices receive low-rank, visit-specific SVD deltas initialized from the released Show-o2 layers. The full calls remain genuine Transformer computation. The middle layout stores more unique parameters than cycle, which is acceptable if it improves image quality at similar executed depth.
2. **Flow-time allocation.** Measure how a fixed amount of loop computation affects early semantic layout and late visual detail. Candidate policies allocate delta rank or further loop computation according to flow time and autoregressive semantic features. The policy must be compared with uniform-rank, uniform-loop, and extra-flow-step baselines at matched latency/FLOPs.
3. **Quality-aware training.** Velocity imitation alone is an initialization objective. The next training objective should include preference or image-level feedback and a proximity term to the pretrained velocity field. Reward training must be audited with an independent preference model and blind human comparison, since optimizing only the evaluation reward can hide artifacts.
4. **Understanding preservation.** Keep the autoregressive reasoning path intact in the first experiment; test both exact logit agreement for the frozen path and standard image-understanding tasks. Later joint tuning is a separate ablation, not an assumed benefit.

## Current evidence and failure modes

- The rank-1024 shared five-block loop uses 144.2M fewer parameters than Show-o2 1.5B, but its 56-image pilot PickScore is 0.452 below the baseline. On 36 untouched prompts it is 0.303 below. This is not yet a quality improvement.
- Rank-1280 SVD initialization has held-out trajectory MSE 0.01533, versus 0.02874 for trained rank-1024. A 1,800-step velocity imitation run **worsens** rank-1280 held-out MSE to 0.02163. Further training requires a changed objective and independent image-level validation.
- A trained first-loop readout lowers trajectory MSE but yields visibly blurred images. Intermediate velocity accuracy is not a sufficient image-quality criterion.
- The rank-1280 error is time dependent (mean MSE 0.01071 at the first sampled state and 0.02911 at the last). This motivates a measured flow-time allocation study; it does not yet prove an optimal schedule.
- On a stratified, one-seed 72-image GenEval subset, rank-1280 cycle at **60** flow steps scores 0.6250 against the original model at the same 60 steps, 0.6389 (paired delta -0.0139, 95% prompt-bootstrap interval [-0.0972, 0.0694]). The small pilot does not establish a difference. Its 50-step score is 0.5972 against a 50-step baseline of 0.6389. Neither setting meets the desired improvement bar.
- `SEQUENCE` at rank 1280 has held-out velocity MSE 0.02248 after SVD initialization, worse than the cycle layout's 0.01533, despite equal ten-block execution. This supports testing loop placement, but generation quality must decide between layouts.
- A 250-step image-level PickScore-guided final-head run completed. Its 100-step checkpoint has held-out velocity MSE 0.01530, essentially unchanged from the 0.01533 initialization. Full-image preference and GenEval measurements are still required; the training reward itself is not evidence of improvement.
- `MIDDLE` reduces held-out velocity MSE to 0.01076 but scores only 0.5833 on the same 50-step GenEval-72 pilot, versus 0.6389 original and 0.5972 `CYCLE`. Its paired PickScore change is -0.1471 on 72 images. Thus trajectory fidelity and generation success are empirically decoupled here, especially for color attribution (0.167 versus 0.50 original). A 14-image qualitative set looks broadly similar but is too small to override the scored result.
- PickScore-guided final-head training at 250 steps scores 22.0779 versus 22.2914 original on a 14-image pilot (paired -0.2135). The reward objective as implemented has not improved full-image quality.

## Decision protocol

First use a stratified GenEval pilot of 72 prompts, 12 from each of six official categories, with paired seeds. Its score is directional because one image per prompt has wide uncertainty. The official 553-prompt, four-image protocol follows only if the pilot supports the hypothesis. At every selection point compare image quality (PickScore plus an independent preference model and blinded human ratings), text alignment, within-prompt diversity, wall-clock time, memory, and GenEval category breakdown. Keep all configurations and seeds, including failures. Do not tune on the final GenEval set.

ImageNet-256 class-conditional FID and UCF-101 class-conditional FVD should be included only with a class-conditional model or a validated conditioning adapter trained on those data. The present Show-o2 text-to-image 432x432 experiment can report same-protocol FID against its own 432x432 text-conditioned baseline, but that number is not numerically comparable to ELT's class-conditional FID 2.0 or FVD 72.8. For a direct ELT comparison, hold the dataset split, image/video resolution, number of samples, conditioning, feature extractor, guidance, and sampling budget fixed, and report both quality and FLOPs/latency. A tiny 72-image GenEval subset must not be used to estimate FID or FVD.

The minimum bar for a main method claim is a positive paired GenEval delta and a positive independent preference delta with no material diversity or understanding regression. A compelling paper additionally needs a clear quality–compute frontier and ablations showing that semantic conditioning and flow-time allocation each contribute beyond SVD initialization, generic looping, and guidance tuning.

## Literature boundary

- [ELT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09168) introduces shared Transformer looping and intra-loop self-distillation for visual generation.
- [Depth through recurrence for flow-matching TTS](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.29768) compares seven reuse layouts at the same 18 executed calls. Its `SEQUENCE` and partially shared `MIDDLE` results motivate controlled placement experiments in our flow head; speech WER findings do not imply image-quality gains.
- [LoopVAE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.11516) reuses a scale/loop-conditioned core inside the visual tokenizer, keeps resolution transitions independent, and shows that useful recurrence does not guarantee useful early exits. Its 0.28 **reconstruction** FID is not a generation FID. A future tokenizer-side intervention should separate the effect of latent quality from generator looping and measure added VAE decode time.
- [Fractal-Guided Approach for Image Generation with Diffus](https://ekmair.ukma.edu.ua/items/929a99dd-a89e-47d1-a7d8-1c7a068ae2f4) describes recursive patch generation with conditional diffusion and depth/timestep-conditioned small hypernetworks. Its cross-scale recursion and visit conditioning suggest a later multi-scale ablation, but require a separate model path; this source is a thesis, so no quantitative result is imported without direct verification.
- [Looped-DiT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40305) already studies full visual loops, intermediate supervision, and quality improvement in text-to-image diffusion. Generic looped visual generation alone is therefore not novel enough.
- [DyDiT++](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.06803) studies timestep-dependent diffusion computation, including flow models. A generic timestep scheduler alone is also insufficient.
- [Show-o2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15564) provides the AR/flow unified generator used here. [Transfusion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11039) is another hybrid setting in which transfer should be tested.
- [Flow-GRPO](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/3a10c46572628d58cb44fb705f25cbbf-Abstract-Conference.html) shows that reward training can improve GenEval for flow models. The planned novelty must come from coupling that training with loop allocation in a hybrid understanding/generation architecture, not merely adding a reward loss.
- [Dreaming in Flow](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08282) already couples image-grounded flow feedback and understanding replay in Show-o2, improving both GenEval and understanding. Bidirectional feedback alone is therefore prior art; the proposed contribution must isolate added value from recurrent depth and its semantic/time-dependent allocation. [RealUnify](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Shi_RealUnify_Do_Unified_Models_Truly_Benefit_from_Unification_A_Comprehensive_CVPR_2026_paper.html) provides a direct bidirectional-synergy benchmark.
- [GenEval](https://github.com/djghosh13/geneval) supplies the object-focused benchmark and official evaluation. [GenEval 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/GenEval2) should be a secondary audit because the original benchmark has documented drift.
